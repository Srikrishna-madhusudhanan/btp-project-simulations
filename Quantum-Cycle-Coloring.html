Where the measurement comes from

The quantum protocol is short. The work in the paper is choosing the POVM {Ma}. The construction has four stages.

  1. A 1-dependent coloring on an infinite space. Take the group G = ⟨g1…gs | gi² = e⟩ with s = 10,000 involutions. A color is a signed generator ±gi, so q = 2s. Iterate Vn+1 = Vn + (2/s)(E − S(Vn)) from V0 = 0. The error contracts by a factor λ each round.
  2. Vectorize and take a square root. Read Vn as a polynomial in ℂ[G×G] and replace it by W², where W is a Taylor series of the square root. Every KaW²Ka is then positive semidefinite.
  3. Evaluate on finite matrices. Send gi to Ui = [[0, OiT],[Oi, 0]], where the Oi are independent Haar-random orthogonal d×d matrices and N = 2d. Map [g,h] to Ug ⊗ conj(Uh). Each Ka becomes a projector, so each operator has zero energy.
  4. Normalize. Ma = T−1/2 M̃a T−1/2 with T = Σa M̃a. For large N, T is close to the identity and the normalization adds only a little energy.

Why s = 10,000

Both bounds are the paper’s sufficient conditions. However the constant can be further optimized.

What a faithful simulation would cost

Iteration count uses Lemma 5.10 with closeness √(ε/7) as in Theorem 5.3, using the s above. The paper gives no explicit N(ε), so N here is just a size to price out.

What the guarantee means

Each edge is monochromatic with probability at most ε, for any ε you choose. The register dimension N grows as ε shrinks. The round count (1) and the number of colors (20,000) stay fixed.

With nodes that know n, set ε = 1/nC+1. A union bound over the n edges gives all colors proper with probability at least 1 − 1/nC.

Against picking colors at random

Baseline: every node outputs a uniformly random color out of 20,000 with no communication, so each edge conflicts with probability 1/20,000.
